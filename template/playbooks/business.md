# Playbook: business — ventures, market entries, Big-Four-style opportunity reviews

## Always

- Catalogue dimensions: **market evidence**, **unit economics**.
- Fixed seats: investor, operator, customer, lawyer, skeptic (+1–2 from triage).
- Prototype means: a one-page model plus five real customer signals (quotes, waitlist entries, LOIs, paid pilots, documented demand — each sourced and dated). Contacting anyone is external contact → human gate.
- Deliverable at Build: the full opportunity review or venture plan as a document (and deck if the brief asks), not software.

## Stage 0

See `playbooks/_meta.md` Stage 0. Business-specific: state the customer, the job-to-be-done, and the one number that must be true for the economics to work.

## Stage 1

Validate.
1. Market size bottom-up (not top-down): who, how many, what they pay today — ≥3 dated sources each. `research.md` cap 120 lines.
2. Competitive set: five closest alternatives incl. "do nothing", with price points and dates.
3. Regulatory or licensing exposure in the target jurisdiction, sourced.
4. Panel → `panel.md`; score → `scores/round-1.md`; `gates/01.md` (hard).

## Stage 2

Plan → `plan.md`: definition of done (what document/deck, for whom, decision it enables), the riskiest assumption, which customer signals to collect and how without external contact until gated, stage budgets, human gates (every interview, survey, paid data source).

## Stage 3

Prototype → `prototype/model.md` (one page: revenue drivers, cost drivers, break-even, sensitivity on the one number) and `prototype/signals.md` (five signals, each sourced). Re-score; steer by score.

## Stage 4

Build → the deliverable in `build/` (markdown source; export formats at Review). Structure for an opportunity review: situation · market · competition · economics · risks · options · recommendation · appendix of sources. Every number has a source and date. Gate before any external contact.

## Stage 5

Review. Panel reads the finished deliverable. Final score. `gates/05.md` (accept). Export the deliverable and the model.
